A standard **company limited by guarantee** (or by shares) registered at
Companies House, with no charitable status. Some clubs use this when they're
running significant commercial operations — large bar trade, function-room
hire, sponsorships — and the charity model doesn't fit.

## Why a club might pick this

- **Limited liability** for directors.
- **No restrictions on trading** — you can run any lawful business.
- **Familiar form** — banks, suppliers, insurers all recognise it instantly.
- **Suitable for shareholder model** if members are putting in capital.

## What you give up

- **No Gift Aid** on member donations.
- **No mandatory rates relief** — pay full business rates unless your
  council offers a discretionary discount.
- **No charity grant access** for most trusts and foundations.
- **Corporation tax** on profits.

## When this is the right choice

Honest answer: rarely, for a typical members' bowls club. You'd usually pick
this only if:

- You have a substantial **commercial trading subsidiary** (function venue,
  catering business) and want to keep it separate from the club.
- You're **converting from a partnership** that's outgrown its structure.
- The **club's primary activity isn't charitable** in the legal sense.

A common pattern is: charity (CIO/SCIO) for the club + wholly-owned trading
subsidiary (limited company) for the bar/function room. The trading
subsidiary gifts its profits up to the charity (Gift Aid), keeping the
charity tax-clean.

## Funding routes

- **Bank lending** — straightforward, you have a recognised structure.
- **Sponsorships** — corporate sponsors are comfortable funding limited
  companies.
- **Crowdfunding** (rewards-based) — Kickstarter / Crowdfunder.
- **Local authority business grants** — when available.

## Compliance

- **Annual confirmation statement** to Companies House.
- **Annual accounts** filed at Companies House.
- **Corporation tax return** to HMRC.
- **PAYE** if you have any paid staff.

This is more paperwork than a CASC or unincorporated association — make sure
the trading scale justifies it.
