A **Scottish Charitable Incorporated Organisation (SCIO)** is the
charity-specific legal form for Scotland. Like a CIO south of the border, it
combines charitable status with limited liability for trustees.

## Who regulates it

The **Office of the Scottish Charity Regulator (OSCR)**. You file an annual
return + accounts via OSCR's online services.

## Why a Scottish club might pick a SCIO

- **Limited liability for trustees** — committee members aren't personally
  liable for the club's debts.
- **Charitable status** — recognised by HMRC for tax relief, eligible for
  Gift Aid, and unlocks Scottish Government / lottery funding routes.
- **Single regulator** — OSCR only; no Companies House requirement.

## Funding routes a SCIO unlocks

- **sportscotland** — investment programmes for clubs and facilities.
- **Scottish Government active schools / community sport funds.**
- **National Lottery — Awards for All Scotland** (up to £20,000).
- **Local authority and Common Good Fund grants** — most require charity
  status.
- **Gift Aid** on member donations.

## Compliance

- **OSCR Annual Return** within 9 months of year end.
- **Receipts & Payments accounts** acceptable below £250,000 income.
- **Trustee statement of public benefit** — short narrative each year.

The platform's **Charity Accounts** module is set up to produce
OSCR-compatible Receipts & Payments. When your form is set to SCIO, the
regulator field is pre-filled to OSCR.

## Converting from an unincorporated association

You can transfer to a SCIO via OSCR's "transfer of assets" route — broadly
similar process to CIO conversion, but check OSCR's current guidance for the
forms and timescales.
