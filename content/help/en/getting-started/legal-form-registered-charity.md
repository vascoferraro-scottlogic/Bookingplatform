A **registered charity** is the traditional charitable form: you're
constituted, you have charitable purposes, and you're on the register kept
by your jurisdiction's regulator.

This article covers the **unincorporated** variant. If you want charity
status *with* limited liability, look at the
[CIO](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-cio) (England & Wales / Northern
Ireland) or [SCIO](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-scio) (Scotland)
instead — most new charities go that route.

## Which regulator?

- **England & Wales** — Charity Commission for England and Wales (CCEW).
- **Scotland** — Office of the Scottish Charity Regulator (OSCR). All
  Scottish charities, including unincorporated, register with OSCR.
- **Northern Ireland** — Charity Commission for Northern Ireland (CCNI).

We'll set the regulator automatically based on the country you picked in
onboarding. You can change it under **Charity Settings** if needed.

## When this form fits

- Long-established clubs that have always operated this way and have no
  reason to convert.
- Very small clubs with minimal contracts and assets, where personal
  trustee liability is a low real-world risk.
- Clubs that want charity status (rate relief, Gift Aid, grant access) but
  don't want a corporate body.

## When it doesn't

- You sign meaningful contracts (premises lease, employee, supplier).
  Trustees are personally on the hook. Convert to a CIO/SCIO instead.
- You hold significant assets (a clubhouse, a green you own). Same
  reason.
- You're starting fresh in 2026. Just go straight to CIO/SCIO — same
  benefits, simpler in the long run.

## Trustee duties

Trustees are personally responsible for the charity acting within its
purposes and within the law. Key duties:

- Act in the charity's best interests.
- Manage resources responsibly.
- Act with reasonable care and skill.
- Ensure compliance with the governing document and the law.

Failing to do this can expose trustees personally — that's the main reason
new charities prefer the incorporated CIO/SCIO route.

## Annual reporting

You must file annual returns with your regulator. Thresholds vary by
jurisdiction but the broad picture:

- **Receipts and Payments accounts** — allowed for smaller charities
  (typically under £250k income E&W; under £250k income SCO; under £250k
  income NI). Cash-basis, much simpler.
- **Accruals (SoFA) accounts** — required above the threshold, or if you
  hold permanent endowment / specific designated funds, or if your
  governing document requires it.

We'll generate Receipts & Payments out of the box once you enable charity
accounts. SoFA support is on the roadmap.

## Public benefit

Every charity must demonstrate **public benefit** — your activities have to
benefit the public (or a sufficient section of it), not just members. For a
sports club this means things like:

- Open membership policy (no excessive subscription, no discriminatory bars).
- Free or subsidised junior coaching.
- Open days, schools partnerships, community use of facilities.
- Disability inclusion.

Document what you do — it'll come up in your annual return.

## Converting to a CIO/SCIO

If you decide the personal liability is no longer worth it, you can convert
an unincorporated charity to a CIO/SCIO. The process is essentially:

1. Set up a new CIO/SCIO with the same purposes.
2. Transfer assets and activities across.
3. Wind up the original.

Your regulator has guidance on this. It's a project of months, not weeks,
but well-trodden.

## Funding routes

Same as for CIOs:

- Grant funders accepting charities.
- Gift Aid on individual donations.
- Mandatory 80% business rate relief on the clubhouse.
- Discretionary further 20% from your council.
- Sport England / sportscotland / Sport Wales / Sport NI funds.
- Lottery community funds.
