An **unincorporated association** is the simplest, oldest, most common form
of club. It's a group of people coming together for a common purpose, with a
written constitution but no separate legal personality.

## How you set one up

- Adopt a **constitution** — a written set of rules covering membership,
  committee elections, AGMs, finances, dissolution. Your governing body
  (Bowls England, Bowls Scotland, etc) usually publishes a model constitution
  you can adapt.
- Hold an inaugural meeting and elect a committee.
- Open a bank account in the club's name (most banks accept clubs).

That's it — no register, no fees, no regulator.

## The catch: personal liability

An unincorporated association **isn't a separate legal person**. Contracts
are signed by the trustees personally. Debts, lawsuits, employment
liabilities — they ultimately land on the committee.

In practice, for a small club with no significant assets, debt, or staff,
this risk is low. Once you start owning a clubhouse, employing groundsmen,
or signing leases, the risk gets real.

## What you can still do as an unincorporated club

- Run regular bowls activity, league matches, social events.
- Charge subscriptions and entry fees.
- Apply for **some** grants — many small grants accept unincorporated clubs.
- Open bank accounts, buy insurance, employ staff (with care).

## What you'll struggle with

- **Most charity-only grants** require registration as a charity.
- **Owning land or buildings** — usually held by trustees personally on the
  club's behalf, which gets messy.
- **Long leases** — landlords prefer incorporated counterparties.
- **Larger contracts** — sponsors and suppliers often want to see a
  registered entity.

## When to convert (and to what)

Convert to a **CIO** (England & Wales), **SCIO** (Scotland), or **CASC**
(tax status only) when:

- Income is regularly above £25,000/year.
- You're about to take on a lease, mortgage, or major contract.
- You want to hire paid staff.
- Trustees express discomfort about personal liability.
- A funder you want requires it.

The conversion process is paperwork-heavy but well-trodden — see the
**CIO** or **SCIO** help articles for the steps.

## Funding open to unincorporated clubs

- **Local authority small grants** — many councils accept unincorporated
  clubs.
- **National Lottery — Awards for All** (up to £10,000 typically open to
  unincorporated clubs).
- **Sport England small grants and Inspired Facilities** (within limits).
- **County / regional bowls associations** — small grants and equipment
  funds.
- **Brewery / bar industry support** — pub-co grants for clubhouse facilities.
