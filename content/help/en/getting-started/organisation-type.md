When you onboard, we ask what legal form your club takes. This drives a
chunk of how the platform behaves — whether we enable charity accounting,
which regulator we tag your records with, what reporting templates you'll
see.

Here's the short version of each option, with a link to a deeper article.

## Charity-style forms

These get charity accounting features turned on automatically, with the
regulator inferred from your country.

- **[CIO](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-cio)** — Charitable
  Incorporated Organisation. Limited liability + charity benefits, regulated
  by Charity Commission (E&W) or CCNI (NI). Most common pick for new
  charities in 2026.
- **[SCIO](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-scio)** — Scottish equivalent
  of a CIO, regulated by OSCR. Two-tier and single-tier forms.
- **[Registered charity (unincorporated)](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-registered-charity)**
  — traditional charity status without limited liability. Trustees take
  personal risk on contracts and debts.
- **[CASC](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-casc)** — Community Amateur
  Sports Club. HMRC tax status, not charity status. Tax reliefs without the
  full charity governance burden. Sits on top of an unincorporated or
  limited-company structure.

## Other forms

These don't get charity accounting auto-enabled, but you can switch it on
manually under **Charity Settings** if your structure includes a charitable
component (e.g. a CIC operating a charitable trust).

- **[CIC](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-cic)** — Community Interest
  Company. Asset lock + community benefit, but not a charity. Useful when
  you trade and want a social-purpose wrapper.
- **[Limited company](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-limited-company)**
  — standard company limited by guarantee or by shares. Corporate clarity
  without charity governance load. Common as a trading subsidiary under a
  charity.
- **[Unincorporated association](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-unincorporated-association)**
  — easiest possible setup, no registration, but trustees personally
  liable. Most clubs start here.
- **[Private members' club](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-private-members-club)**
  — formal members' club, often with a clubhouse and bar licence. Not
  charitable; specific tax treatment for members' subs.
- **Other** — if none of the above fits, pick this. We won't enable any
  jurisdiction-specific features, and you can configure things manually.
- **[Not yet constituted](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-not-constituted)**
  — pre-formal stage. The article walks you through how to get from "we
  meet at the green on Tuesdays" to constituted-and-bankable.

## Why we ask

- **Country + organisation type** together determine whether to enable
  charity accounting (Receipts & Payments, fund tracking, regulator
  reporting). E.g. CIO in Scotland → SCIO/OSCR; CIO in NI → CCNI; CIO in
  England → CCEW.
- **Financial year end** drives report periods, accruals, fiscal-year
  comparison charts. We lift this up to the tenant level so charity
  accounts and any future trading-subsidiary accounts share it by default.

## Can I change this later?

Yes. Under **Settings → Organisation** you can change:

- Country
- Organisation type
- Financial year end

Switching from a non-charitable form to a charitable form will offer to
enable charity accounting. Switching the other way doesn't disable it
automatically — too easy to lose data — you can turn the feature off
manually if you really want to.

## Still not sure?

If you're at the "we have a constitution but never registered" stage, the
[unincorporated association](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-unincorporated-association)
article is probably what fits you today, with the
[CIO](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-cio) /
[SCIO](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-scio) /
[CASC](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-casc) articles describing where
you might end up.

If you're at the "we don't even have a constitution" stage, start with the
[Not yet constituted](/dashboard/help/article/legal-form-not-constituted)
article.
