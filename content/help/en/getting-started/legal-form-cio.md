A **Charitable Incorporated Organisation (CIO)** is the modern, charity-specific
legal form for England & Wales. It gives you the public-benefit status of a
charity *and* limited liability for your trustees — the best of both worlds.

## Who regulates it

The **Charity Commission for England & Wales** (CC E&W). You file an annual
return + accounts each year via their online portal.

## Why a club might pick a CIO

- **Limited liability for trustees** — your committee aren't personally on the
  hook if the club is sued or runs up debt.
- **Charitable status** — eligible for charitable rate relief, Gift Aid, and
  most grant funders' "must be a registered charity" filters.
- **Single regulator** — you don't also need Companies House, unlike a
  charitable limited company.
- **No share capital** — clean fit for a members' association.

## Funding routes a CIO unlocks

- **Sport England** — most of their National Lottery and Place Partnerships
  funding is open to constituted clubs; CIOs satisfy the criteria.
- **Local authority small grants** — councils prefer (and sometimes require)
  a charity number.
- **Gift Aid on member donations and subscriptions** — claim 25% on top of
  qualifying gifts from UK taxpayers.
- **National Lottery Community Fund** — Awards for All up to £20,000.
- **Trusts and foundations** — many will only fund registered charities.

## Compliance to be aware of

- **Annual return + accounts** — Receipts & Payments format is fine while
  income is below £250,000.
- **Trustee declarations** — can't have unspent convictions for dishonesty or
  bankruptcy without permission.
- **Public benefit reporting** — short statement in your annual return on how
  your activities benefit the public.

The platform's **Charity Accounts** module is set up to produce the Receipts
& Payments table you'll need. Your charity number, regulator, and year-end
will be pre-filled when you complete onboarding.

## Converting to a CIO from another form

Coming from an unincorporated association? You'll need to:

1. Set up a new CIO with the Charity Commission (free; takes ~6 weeks).
2. Transfer assets from the old club to the new CIO.
3. Wind up the old association cleanly.

It's a paperwork exercise, not a legal minefield — but get advice from your
governing body (Bowls England, Bowls Scotland, etc) or a charity solicitor
before you start moving money.
