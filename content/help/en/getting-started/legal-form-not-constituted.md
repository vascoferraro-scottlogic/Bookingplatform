If you've answered **"Not yet constituted"** in onboarding, this article is
for you. Running an organised club without a legal form is doable, but every
contract, grant application, and bank conversation will hit a wall sooner or
later.

The good news: getting constituted is mostly paperwork, not law school.
Here's the route most clubs take.

## Step 1 — Decide your form

For a typical bowls club, your three real options are:

- **Unincorporated association** — fastest, free, simplest. Trustees take
  personal liability. Good for clubs without major assets or contracts.
- **Charitable Incorporated Organisation (CIO/SCIO)** — limited liability
  + charity benefits. The right answer for most growing clubs.
- **CASC (HMRC tax status)** — sits on top of an unincorporated or limited
  company structure. Tax reliefs without the full charity governance load.

If you're not sure, **start unincorporated** and convert to CIO/SCIO/CASC
when income, asset value, or risk justifies it. Many clubs follow exactly
this path.

## Step 2 — Adopt a constitution

A **constitution** (or "rules") is a written document covering:

- Name and purpose of the club.
- Membership categories and fees.
- Committee structure and elections.
- Powers of the AGM.
- Financial year end and accounting expectations.
- Dissolution clause (where assets go if the club closes — for a charity, to
  another charity with similar aims).

**Don't write one from scratch.** Use a model constitution from your
governing body:

- **Bowls England** — model constitution for affiliated clubs.
- **Bowls Scotland** — model constitution and SCIO templates.
- **Bowls Wales / WIBA / NIBA** — equivalent templates.
- **NCVO** (England) / **SCVO** (Scotland) / **NICVA** (Northern Ireland) —
  generic charity model constitutions.

Adapt the model for your club, then adopt it at an inaugural meeting.

## Step 3 — Inaugural meeting

- Convene interested members.
- Adopt the constitution by vote.
- Elect first committee (chair, secretary, treasurer at minimum).
- Set initial subscription level.
- Minute everything.

## Step 4 — Open a bank account

With the constitution and minutes, walk into your bank and open a club
account. Most banks have a community / club account product. You'll need
two or three signatories from the committee.

## Step 5 — Insurance

You need at least:

- **Public liability insurance** — third-party injury / damage on your
  premises or at events.
- **Trustees' liability** if you're unincorporated and feeling exposed.

Your governing body usually offers a club-specific insurance scheme at
favourable rates.

## Step 6 — Register, if applicable

- **CIO/SCIO** — apply via Charity Commission / OSCR website. Free. ~6
  weeks.
- **CASC** — apply via HMRC. Free. Decision typically within 3 months.
- **Limited company / CIC** — incorporate at Companies House. £12-£35.

## Step 7 — Affiliate

- Affiliate to your county / regional / national governing body. This
  unlocks competition entry, insurance discounts, and small grant routes.

## How long does all this take?

- **Constitution + inaugural meeting**: a couple of weeks of organising.
- **Bank account**: 2-4 weeks.
- **Charity registration**: ~6 weeks (CIO) to several months.
- **CASC**: ~3 months.

You can run the club operationally from the inaugural meeting onward — you
don't have to wait for charity / CASC paperwork to start playing bowls.

## What about funding right now?

While you're getting constituted:

- **Crowdfunding** (Kickstarter / Crowdfunder) doesn't require any
  particular legal form.
- **Local sponsors** — small businesses can sponsor an unconstituted group
  via a named individual.
- **Friends-of-the-club fundraising** — informal, but keep records.

But hold off on **applying to grant funders** until you've at least adopted
a constitution and opened a club bank account. You'll be turned down
without these.

## Where to get help

- **Your sport's governing body** — the single best starting point.
- **NCVO / SCVO / NICVA** — third-sector support organisations with free
  governance advice.
- **Local Council for Voluntary Service (CVS)** — most areas have one;
  free advice on setting up.
- **Charity Commission / OSCR / CCNI** — official guidance pages on their
  websites.
